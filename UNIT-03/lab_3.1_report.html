DATA 401 - Lab 3.1

PART 1 - Business Question and Decision Framing

1.1 What is working? Which product categories and sub-categories generate the most sales, and do those sales also generate profit?

1.2 What is not working? Which products or categories have low sales or negative profit, and are high discounts associated with weaker results?

2 Management decision: Review pricing, discounting, and assortment for product lines with losses, especially Tables and Bookcases, before changing inventory or promotions. Keep investment in profitable categories while testing any changes on a limited scale.

3 Decision type: A little decision. The evidence supports a targeted review and small, reversible pricing or promotion tests; it does not justify a company-wide strategy change by itself.

PART 2 - Data Assessment

1 Data Quality. The Orders sheet contains 10,194 order-line records and has no blank cells in the fields checked. Row ID is unique and there are no fully duplicated rows; repeated Order IDs are expected because an order can contain multiple products. Sales, quantity, discount, and profit are populated and numeric. Accuracy cannot be confirmed without comparing records with source transactions. The extract spans Jan. 3, 2023 to Dec. 30, 2026; because the latest date is later than the current date (Oct. 20, 2026), confirm whether those future-dated records are forecasts, test data, or a date issue. The workbook is a static sample, so its update date and currentness are unknown.

2 Generalizability. The results describe this fictional Superstore sample, which covers U.S. orders and three customer segments. They can suggest questions for a similar retailer, but should not be treated as representative of all retailers, other countries, or current operations. The dataset is not a random sample of the broader retail market.

3 Meaning. Each row is a product line within an order, not a unique customer or whole order. Sales is the recorded dollar value for that line; Profit is a separate dollar measure and can be negative. Correct interpretation needs the currency, accounting definitions, time period, discount conventions, and context on whether the rows represent completed sales, returns, or other adjustments. The workbook includes a separate Returns sheet, which should be joined or checked before treating these figures as net sales.

4 Ambiguity. (a) Discount is stored as a fraction (for example, 0.20 means 20%), so it can be mistaken for a dollar amount or percentage unless labeled clearly. (b) “Sales” may mean gross line sales rather than net revenue after returns; the separate Returns sheet makes this distinction important. (c) A repeated Order ID may look like a duplicate, although each row can represent a different product line in that order.

PART 4 - Data Visualization and Storytelling

Bar Chart

Title: Category-wise frequency by sub-category. X-axis: Category. Y-axis: Frequency (number of order lines). Each colored bar represents a sub-category. Office Supplies contains the most line records and the greatest variety of frequent product lines; Technology has fewer line records overall. This chart compares transaction-line frequency, not sales dollars or profit.

Scatter Plot

Title: Sales and discount by category. X-axis: Sales dollars per line. Y-axis: Discount rate (fraction; 0.20 = 20%). Each point is a product line, colored by category. Points cluster at common discount levels, while sales have a long right tail. The fitted category lines differ, but overlapping observations and extreme sales values mean this chart alone cannot establish that discount causes a change in sales or profit; profit and returns should be examined before changing policy.

Part 5 – Insight, 80–20 Thinking, and Data Ethics (3 marks)

  1. Business insight: Sales are concentrated across products: the top 20% of product names by total sales contribute about 76.6% of all sales in this dataset. This is substantial concentration, though it does not reach a strict 80% threshold.
  2. Ethical consideration: Customer names and IDs are personal or identifying information. Restrict access to those fields and use aggregated or de-identified results in reports; avoid making decisions about individual customers without a fair, validated basis.
  3. Recommendation: Review discounts, costs, and return rates for loss-making sub-categories before launching promotions. Tables show about $17,753 in aggregate losses and Bookcases about $3,632; test revised pricing or promotion limits on a small group, then compare profit and sales with a baseline before expanding.
